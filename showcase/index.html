How they are made

The recipe

No game engine to install and nothing to download. Almost every game here is built from the same four ingredients.

  1. 01

    One web page

    The whole game is a single HTML file, or a small folder of them. Open it, share it, upload it to itch.io.

  2. 02

    Three.js for 3D

    The free 3D library most of these games use. It is already installed in your starter, next to Phaser for 2D.

  3. 03

    Art made by code

    Shapes, textures and animation are generated when the page loads, so there are no pictures or 3D models to make.

  4. 04

    Sound made by code

    Music and effects are synthesized in the browser. You describe the mood; the model writes the synthesizer.

In the starter, the 3D example is src/three-game.ts and the 2D one is src/game.ts. Ask your AI tool to change one thing and watch what happens.

Before you plan your weekend

Reality check

The numbers here are what each creator reported. The showpieces are not weekend projects, and that is fine.

Showpieces cost real money

Saltline ran for 8 hours and about $550 at API prices. Tidewater's first demo used $1,874 of tokens on a $200 subscription.

"One prompt" is rarely the whole story

Saltline's posted version has two more hours of fixes. The voxel engine needed steering. Hogwarts Royale took a couple of weeks.

Not everything is made by code

What the Water Keeps ships about 60 model files and AI-generated music. Tidewater uses scanned props and recorded audio.

Small is the right size

SUBLEVEL 07 took 40.8 minutes and $15.96. Knock It Off took 1 h 20 m. Finals Night table tennis took about an hour.

Your weekend game will be smaller than Saltline. Aim for one small idea that is fun in the first ten seconds.
